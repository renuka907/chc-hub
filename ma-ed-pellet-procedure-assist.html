1. Overview: What Are Hormone Pellets?

Hormone pellets are small, rice-grain-sized implants containing bioidentical hormones — either estradiol (E2) or testosterone (T) — that are inserted subcutaneously, typically in the upper buttock/hip area. They provide a steady, consistent release of hormones over 3-6 months, avoiding the peaks and valleys of other delivery methods.

Key Facts MAs Should Know

  • Estradiol pellets: Typically 12.5-50 mg for women. Used for menopausal symptoms (hot flashes, mood, sleep, vaginal dryness)
  • Testosterone pellets for women: Typically 50-200 mg. Used for low libido, fatigue, brain fog, muscle loss
  • Testosterone pellets for men: Typically 600-1600 mg. Used for low T symptoms (fatigue, ED, low libido, mood changes)
  • Combination: Many female patients receive both E2 + T pellets in the same visit
  • Duration: Women typically 3-5 months between insertions; men 4-6 months
  • Insertion site: Upper outer quadrant of the buttock (gluteal/hip fat pad)
Key Takeaway

Pellets are compounded, not FDA-approved for women. This is important context — patients sometimes ask. The provider handles this conversation, but you should be aware.

2. Pre-Procedure Responsibilities

A. Room Setup Checklist

Set up the procedure room BEFORE the patient arrives. Everything should be ready and organized on the sterile tray.

Sterile Tray Contents

  • Sterile drape (fenestrated or plain)
  • Trocar and obturator (correct size — typically 3.2mm or 4.5mm)
  • Scalpel blade (#11) or dermal punch
  • Hormone pellets (correct type and dose — verify with provider order)
  • Local anesthetic: Lidocaine 1% with epinephrine (typically 5-10 mL)
  • Syringe (10 mL) + needle for anesthetic (25G for injection, 18G to draw up)
  • Sterile 4x4 gauze (multiple packs)
  • Skin prep (chlorhexidine or betadine swabs)
  • Steri-strips or Dermabond
  • Pressure bandage / Tegaderm + gauze
  • Sterile gloves (provider's size)
  • Non-sterile gloves (for MA)
  • Sharps container (in room)
  • Biohazard bag

Additional Room Needs

  • Patient gown or draping for privacy
  • Pillow for patient comfort (they'll be lying prone or on their side)
  • Ice pack (for post-procedure if requested)
  • Post-procedure care instruction handout (printed and ready)
  • Procedure consent form (if not already signed)

3. During the Procedure

A. Sterile Field Rules

  • Never reach over the sterile field — always go around
  • Never turn your back to the sterile tray
  • If you contaminate anything — speak up immediately. It's always better to replace an item than to risk infection
  • Keep sterile items above waist level
  • Only sterile items touch sterile items
  • If in doubt, consider it contaminated

B. Assisting the Provider

  • Anticipate needs: Have the next instrument ready before the provider asks
  • Typical order: Skin prep → drape → anesthetic → wait 2-3 min → scalpel/incision → trocar → pellets loaded → insertion → closure
  • Hand instruments handle-first to the provider
  • Open sterile packages by peeling the edges without touching the contents
  • Count pellets with the provider — verify all pellets were inserted

C. Patient Comfort & Monitoring

Clinical Pearl

Talk to the patient! Many patients are anxious. Distraction helps. Ask about their weekend, their kids, their pets. Keep the conversation light and natural. Monitor for vasovagal signs: sudden pallor, sweating, nausea, feeling faint. If you notice these, alert the provider immediately — have the patient lie flat and elevate legs if possible.

4. Post-Procedure Care Instructions

Give the patient the printed post-care instruction sheet AND verbally review the key points:

Wound Care

  • Pressure bandage: Keep on for 24 hours. Do not remove early.
  • Steri-strips: Leave in place until they fall off on their own (5-7 days). Do not pull them off.
  • Keep dry: No showering over the site for 24-48 hours
  • No submerging: No baths, pools, hot tubs, or ocean for 5-7 days (infection risk)

Activity Restrictions

  • No strenuous lower body exercise for 5-7 days (squats, lunges, running, cycling)
  • No heavy lifting (>20 lbs) for 5-7 days
  • Light walking is OK after 24 hours
  • Upper body exercise is OK after 48 hours
  • Sitting: OK immediately, but avoid prolonged direct pressure on the insertion site for first 24 hours (sit slightly to the opposite side)

What's Normal vs. What's NOT

Normal (No Need to Call)Call the Office
Mild bruising around the siteIncreasing redness or warmth spreading beyond the bandage area
Tenderness/soreness for 2-5 daysSwelling that's getting worse after 48 hours
Small amount of bleeding on bandagePus or cloudy drainage from the site
Slight firmness under the skin (pellets)Fever (>100.4F)

Important

If a patient calls about pellet extrusion (they can feel a pellet coming through the skin or see it), instruct them to keep it clean and dry, cover with a bandage, and come in as soon as possible. If the pellet has fallen out, tell them to save it in a clean bag and bring it to the office.

5. Follow-Up Scheduling

Appointment TypeWhen to SchedulePurpose
Post-insertion labs4-6 weeks after insertionCheck peak hormone levels (E2, total T, free T)
Symptom follow-up6-8 weeks after insertionHow is the patient feeling? Symptom improvement?
Next reinsertion3-5 months (women) / 4-6 months (men)Before levels drop too low — schedule proactively
Trough labsJust before next reinsertionCheck levels at their lowest to guide dosing
Clinical Pearl

Schedule the next reinsertion BEFORE the patient leaves today. If you wait, patients often forget and call when they're already symptomatic. Proactive scheduling keeps patients on track and reduces phone calls.

6. Common Patient Questions

QuestionMA-Appropriate Response
"Will it hurt?""The provider uses a local anesthetic to numb the area, so you'll feel a pinch with the numbing injection but shouldn't feel the pellet insertion. Most patients say it's much easier than they expected!"
"How long until I feel the effects?""Most patients start noticing improvements in 2-4 weeks. Full effects can take 6-8 weeks. Everyone's timeline is a little different."
"Can I exercise after?""Light walking is fine after 24 hours. We ask that you avoid intense lower body exercise like squats, running, or cycling for 5-7 days to let the site heal."
"What if a pellet comes out?""Extrusion is rare, but if you notice a hard lump pushing through or a pellet comes out, keep the area clean, save the pellet, and call us right away."
"Can I sit normally?""Yes! Just try to avoid putting direct pressure on the exact insertion spot for the first 24 hours. You can shift your weight slightly to the other side."
"Will I gain weight on pellets?""That's a great question for the provider. Testosterone can actually help with body composition — many patients find it helps build lean muscle and reduce fat."
"How do I know when I need them again?""We'll schedule your follow-up labs and next insertion before you leave today. If your symptoms start returning before your scheduled appointment, call us and we can adjust the timing."
Key Takeaway

It's OK to answer practical questions (wound care, scheduling, what to expect physically). For medical questions about dosing, side effects, or whether pellets are right for them — redirect to the provider: "That's a great question — I'll make sure the provider addresses that with you."

7. Complication Recognition & Escalation

Know When to Escalate

Your job is to recognize and report, not to diagnose or treat. When in doubt, alert the provider.

ComplicationSignsAction
InfectionIncreasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus/drainage, fever, red streakingNotify provider IMMEDIATELY. Patient may need antibiotics. If red streaking — urgent evaluation needed.
Pellet extrusionPellet visible or palpable pushing through skin, small hard object at incision siteHave patient come in ASAP. Save any extruded pellet. Provider will assess and may need to re-insert.
HematomaLarge area of bruising/swelling, expanding beyond normal limits, painApply direct pressure and ice. Notify provider. Usually resolves on its own but may need evaluation.
Vasovagal response (during procedure)Sudden pallor, diaphoresis, nausea, feeling faint, slow heart rateAlert provider immediately. Lay patient flat, elevate legs, offer cool cloth, reassure. Monitor vitals.
Allergic reaction (to lidocaine — rare)Hives, facial swelling, difficulty breathing, throat tightnessCALL 911 immediately. This is anaphylaxis. Provider will administer epinephrine. Keep patient calm.

8. Quick Reference Checklists

Pre-Procedure Checklist

  • Procedure room set up with all supplies on sterile tray
  • Consent form signed and in chart
  • Vitals taken and documented
  • Allergies confirmed (especially lidocaine)
  • Blood thinner / NSAID use reviewed
  • Pregnancy test completed if applicable
  • Recent labs confirmed on file
  • Pellet type, dose, and quantity verified with provider
  • Pellet lot numbers and expiration dates recorded
  • Patient positioned comfortably
  • Post-care instruction sheet printed and ready

Post-Procedure Checklist

  • Procedure documented (pellets, doses, lot numbers, site, complications)
  • Patient given verbal post-care instructions
  • Printed post-care handout given to patient
  • Follow-up labs scheduled (4-6 weeks)
  • Next reinsertion appointment scheduled
  • Patient has office phone number for questions/concerns
  • Room cleaned and restocked for next procedure
  • Sharps properly disposed
  • Biohazard waste removed

Post-Care Instructions Summary (For Patient)

  • Pressure bandage on for 24 hours
  • Steri-strips: leave until they fall off (5-7 days)
  • Keep insertion site dry for 24-48 hours
  • No baths, pools, hot tubs for 5-7 days
  • No intense lower body exercise for 5-7 days
  • Light walking OK after 24 hours
  • Mild bruising and soreness is normal
  • Call us if: increasing redness, swelling, drainage, fever, or pellet pushing through skin
