Suicide Risk Assessment & Intervention Protocol

Contemporary Health Center — Provider Reference

⚠ IF PATIENT IS IN IMMEDIATE DANGER: Call 911 — Do NOT leave patient alone
Step 1: Immediate Assessment (Ask Directly)
Questions to Ask
  • "Are you thinking about killing yourself?" (direct is best)
  • "Are you thinking about hurting yourself?"
  • "Have you ever attempted suicide?"
  • "Do you have a plan? If so, what is it?"
  • "Do you have access to means?" (firearms, medications, etc.)
  • "What is keeping you from acting on these thoughts?"
Warning Signs to Observe
  • Talking about being a burden or having no reason to live
  • Withdrawing from friends, family, activities
  • Giving away possessions
  • Sudden calmness after period of depression
  • Increased alcohol/substance use
  • Searching online for methods
  • Recent major loss, trauma, or humiliation
Step 2: Risk Stratification
● Low Risk
  • Passive ideation ("I wish I weren't here")
  • No plan or intent
  • No prior attempts
  • Strong protective factors
  • Willing to engage in safety planning
● Moderate Risk
  • Active ideation with vague plan
  • No immediate intent to act
  • Prior attempt(s) > 1 year ago
  • Some protective factors
  • Willing to contract for safety
● High / Imminent Risk
  • Active ideation with specific plan
  • Intent and/or timeline stated
  • Access to lethal means
  • Recent attempt or rehearsal
  • Refuses help / no protective factors
Step 3: Intervention by Risk Level
Low Risk
  • Complete safety plan with patient (see below)
  • Provide crisis resources (988, crisis text line)
  • Address underlying condition (depression, anxiety, etc.)
  • Schedule follow-up within 1–2 weeks
  • Document assessment and plan in chart
Moderate Risk
  • All low-risk interventions plus:
  • Consider same-day behavioral health warm handoff
  • Counsel on means restriction (lock up firearms, meds)
  • Contact support person with patient’s consent
  • Consider starting/adjusting medication
  • Follow-up within 48–72 hours
High / Imminent Risk
  • Do NOT leave patient alone
  • Remove access to any means in the clinic
  • Call 911 or arrange ED transport
  • Contact emergency psychiatric services
  • Notify emergency contact / next of kin
  • If patient attempts to leave: clearly document AMA, provide crisis resources, call 911 if imminent danger
  • Complete incident report per clinic policy
  • Provider debrief after event

Safety Plan Template (Complete with Patient)
  1. Warning signs — thoughts, feelings, or behaviors that signal a crisis is developing
  2. Internal coping strategies — things patient can do alone to take their mind off problems (walk, music, breathing exercises)
  3. People & social settings that provide distraction — friends, family, or places to go
  4. People to contact for help — name & phone number of 2–3 trusted people
  5. Professionals & agencies to contact — therapist, prescriber, crisis line, ED
  6. Making the environment safe — remove/lock up firearms, medications, sharps; identify who will help
  7. My reason for living — what matters most to the patient (children, pets, goals, faith)
