1. What Are These Hormones? (The Basics)

Total Testosterone (TT)

What it is: The TOTAL amount of testosterone circulating in the blood — both the testosterone that's "busy" (bound to proteins) and the testosterone that's "free" to work.

Think of it as: Your total bank balance — it tells you everything in the account, but not how much you can actually spend right now.

Normal range (adult males): 300–1,000 ng/dL (varies by lab)

Made by: ~95% from the testes (Leydig cells), ~5% from the adrenal glands

Free Testosterone (FT)

What it is: The small fraction (~2-3%) of testosterone that is NOT bound to any protein. This is the testosterone that's actually available to enter cells and DO things — build muscle, maintain libido, support mood, etc.

Think of it as: The cash in your wallet — it's what you can actually use right now.

Normal range (adult males): 5–21 ng/dL (or 1–3% of total T)

Why it matters: A man can have a "normal" total T but STILL feel terrible if his free T is low. This is the number that most closely correlates with symptoms.

SHBG (Sex Hormone-Binding Globulin)

What it is: A protein made by the liver that grabs onto testosterone and holds it tight. Once testosterone is bound to SHBG, it can't enter cells — it's basically locked up.

Think of it as: A vault that locks up your money. The more SHBG you have, the more of your testosterone is locked away and unavailable.

Normal range (adult males): 10–57 nmol/L

The relationship: SHBG up = Free T down (more locked up) · SHBG down = Free T up (more available)

Clinical Pearl — The Pizza Delivery Analogy (Great for Patients)

Total Testosterone = All the pizza you ordered tonight.
SHBG-bound Testosterone = The pizzas still locked in the delivery car. You paid for them, they're yours, but you can't eat them yet.
Albumin-bound Testosterone = Pizzas sitting on the counter — loosely held, can be grabbed if needed (this is "bioavailable" T).
Free Testosterone = The slice in your hand. That's the one actually feeding you.

2. How They Work Together

About 98% of circulating testosterone is bound to proteins:

Key Takeaway — Bioavailable Testosterone

= Free T + Albumin-bound T. Some providers prefer this measure because albumin holds testosterone loosely and it can detach easily at the tissue level. Think of it as "spendable money" — both the cash in your wallet AND the money in your easily-accessible checking account.

Clinical Pearl

This is why you should ALWAYS check Total T, Free T, AND SHBG together. Total T alone can be misleading. A man with Total T of 500 ng/dL and high SHBG might have less usable testosterone than a man with Total T of 400 ng/dL and low SHBG.

3. What Makes SHBG Go Up or Down?

Things That RAISE SHBG (= Less Free T)
  • Aging — SHBG increases ~1-2% per year after 40
  • Liver disease (cirrhosis, hepatitis)
  • Hyperthyroidism
  • Anticonvulsants (phenytoin, carbamazepine)
  • HIV
  • Estrogen therapy (exogenous)
  • Anorexia / severe calorie restriction
  • Excessive alcohol use
  • High-fiber / vegetarian diets
Things That LOWER SHBG (= More Free T)
  • Obesity — especially visceral fat
  • Type 2 Diabetes / Insulin Resistance
  • Hypothyroidism
  • Metabolic syndrome
  • Nephrotic syndrome
  • Exogenous androgens / anabolic steroids
  • Corticosteroids
  • Growth hormone use
  • PCOS medications (in context)
Warning — The Obesity Paradox

Obese men often have LOW SHBG (which should mean more free T), BUT they also have lower total T production AND higher aromatase activity converting T to estrogen. So they can end up with low total T, low-ish free T, AND low SHBG — a triple mess. Always look at the full picture.

Key Takeaway — The #1 Missed Diagnosis

Normal Total T + Low Free T + High SHBG. This patient walks into his PCP's office, gets a total T of 550, and is told "you're normal." But he feels exhausted, has no sex drive, brain fog, and can't build muscle. His free T is 4.2 and SHBG is 72. He's functionally hypogonadal despite a "normal" total T. This is why we check the full panel.

5. Symptoms of Low vs. High Testosterone

Low Testosterone Symptoms

Sexual:

  • Low or absent libido
  • Erectile dysfunction
  • Reduced morning erections
  • Decreased ejaculate volume

Physical:

  • Fatigue / low energy
  • Loss of muscle mass & strength
  • Increased body fat (especially belly)
  • Decreased bone density
  • Gynecomastia (breast tissue)
  • Hair loss (body hair thinning)
  • Hot flashes / night sweats

Mental/Emotional:

  • Brain fog / poor concentration
  • Depression / irritability
  • Poor motivation
  • Sleep disturbances
  • Memory issues
High Testosterone Symptoms (Usually from TRT/Steroids)

Cardiovascular:

  • Polycythemia (elevated H&H) — clot risk
  • Elevated blood pressure
  • Edema / fluid retention

Skin:

  • Acne (back, shoulders, face)
  • Oily skin
  • Accelerated male pattern baldness

Behavioral:

  • Irritability / aggression
  • Mood swings
  • Sleep apnea worsening

Reproductive:

  • Testicular atrophy (on exogenous T)
  • Infertility (suppressed LH/FSH)
  • Elevated estradiol (aromatization)

Lab Red Flags:

  • Hematocrit >54% — phlebotomy needed
  • PSA elevation — urology referral

6. How to Explain It to Patients (Scripts)

Script — Explaining Total vs. Free Testosterone

"Think of your total testosterone like your total paycheck. That's the big number. But not all of that money is available to spend — some goes to taxes, some goes to bills that are automatically taken out. Your FREE testosterone is like your take-home pay. That's what actually hits your pocket and what your body can use day to day. Right now, your take-home pay is pretty low, even though your gross paycheck looks okay."

Script — Explaining High SHBG

"You have this protein called SHBG — think of it like a sponge that soaks up your testosterone. The more SHBG you have, the more testosterone gets soaked up and becomes unavailable. Your SHBG is running high right now, which means even though you're producing a decent amount of testosterone, a lot of it is getting gobbled up before your body can use it. That's why you're feeling the way you do."

Script — Explaining Why Total T Alone Is Misleading

"When your last doctor checked your testosterone and said it was 'normal,' they were looking at the total number. And technically, yes, it is in the normal range. But that's like saying your bank account has $5,000 in it without mentioning that $4,500 of it is frozen. The amount that's actually available and working in your body — your free testosterone — is low. That's what's causing your symptoms."

Script — Explaining Low T to a Skeptical Patient

"I know there's a lot of stuff on the internet about testosterone, and some of it makes it sound like a scam or just a 'feel good' thing. But testosterone is a real hormone with real medical effects. When your levels are genuinely low, it affects your energy, your mood, your bones, your heart health, your ability to build and maintain muscle. This isn't about bodybuilding — it's about your body having what it needs to function properly."

Warning — Red Flags — When to Act
  • Hematocrit >54%: Hold TRT, therapeutic phlebotomy, recheck in 4 weeks
  • PSA >4.0 or rapid rise (>1.4 ng/mL/year): Urology referral
  • Estradiol >40 pg/mL with symptoms: Consider anastrozole 0.25-0.5mg 2x/week
  • Total T >1,200 or Free T significantly above range: Reduce dose
  • New sleep apnea symptoms: Sleep study referral

8. Quick Reference Card

The 60-Second Assessment
1
Check Total T — Is it in range (300-1,000)?
2
Check Free T — Is it actually adequate (10 ng/dL or above)?
3
Check SHBG — Is it driving free T down (>50) or is it low (<20)?
4
If SHBG is high — WHY? (age, liver, thyroid, meds, diet)
5
If SHBG is low — WHY? (obesity, diabetes, insulin resistance)
6
Match labs to symptoms — treat the patient, not the number
Remember

Total T tells you production. Free T tells you function. SHBG tells you why they don't always match. Always check all three. Always correlate with symptoms. The numbers tell a story — learn to read it.
