How to Use This Guide

This guide organizes all recommended preventive screenings and vaccinations by age group for your patient population. Use it during annual well-woman visits and primary care encounters to ensure nothing is missed.

USPSTF A = High certainty of substantial benefit USPSTF B = High certainty of moderate benefit RISK-BASED = Based on risk factors VACCINE = CDC/ACIP recommended

Key Sources

  • USPSTF — US Preventive Services Task Force (A & B recommendations)
  • CDC/ACIP — Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (vaccine schedule Oct 2025)
  • ACOG — American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists

Pregnancy-Specific

  • Tdap — 1 dose every pregnancy at 27-36 weeks gestation
  • RSV vaccine — Seasonal (Sep-Jan), 32-36 weeks gestation, OR give baby nirsevimab after birth
  • Flu vaccine — safe and recommended at any trimester
  • GBS screen — 36-37 weeks (ACOG)
  • Gestational diabetes — screen at 24-28 weeks (USPSTF B)
  • Rh(D) typing — first prenatal visit
  • Hep B — screen at first prenatal visit (USPSTF A)
  • Bacteriuria — urine culture screen (USPSTF B)
  • Preeclampsia — BP monitoring throughout; low-dose aspirin 81mg if high risk after 12 wks (USPSTF B)

2024 USPSTF Update: Mammography now starts at 40

The USPSTF updated their breast cancer screening recommendation in April 2024: biennial screening mammography for ALL women aged 40-74. Previously, screening before 50 was an individual decision. This is now a Grade B recommendation for ages 40-74.

When to STOP Screening

  • Cervical cancer (Pap/HPV) — Stop at 65 if adequate prior screening (3 consecutive normal Paps or 2 negative co-tests in last 10 years)
  • Breast cancer (Mammogram) — Current USPSTF recommendation goes through age 74; beyond 74 = insufficient evidence
  • Colorectal cancer — Routine through 75; ages 76-85 individualized; stop at 85
  • Lung cancer — Stop at 80 or if quit smoking more than 15 years ago

PSA Screening Note

The USPSTF gives PSA screening a Grade C (individualized decision) for men 55-69. It's NOT routinely recommended but should be offered after shared decision-making discussion. Grade D (against) for men 70 and older. ACS recommends discussion starting at 50 (or 40-45 for high-risk: African American, family history).
